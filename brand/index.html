What we learned from the publications

Every rule here comes from looking closely at how our magazines and brochures are laid out.

Art lives in circlesCircles of different sizes, sometimes overlapping. Never a plain rectangle when a circle will do.
Edges fade into whiteArt dissolves into the page, often with a soft sea-glass circle behind it.
One topic, one paletteEach piece of art uses a single duotone family, with grain and halftone dots.
Coral leads the eyeHeadings and references are coral. Everything else stays dark and quiet.
White space is contentPages breathe. Nothing is crowded, even on a small phone.
Covers speak in capitalsA colour block with spaced capitals: light words first, then the bold ones.

Art goes here
  • Welcome screens and first-time setup
  • Headers that fade into the page
  • Empty screens, waiting, and "done" moments
  • Small circles that tell tools apart
  • Between parts on the stage screen
Art stays out
  • Behind numbers or buttons people must read fast
  • On the stage screen while someone is speaking
  • In error messages: clear words come first
  • Anywhere it would push the main button off screen
  • More than one large piece on a screen
